:::meta title: Keeping a starter lang: en date: 2026-09-14 description: Feeding the starter, and building a levain from it the night before a bake. ::: # Keeping a starter > A note of the [bread notes](index.html), an example of the wiki add-on. It defines its terms > with `:dfn[…]`, one of them inside `:definition[…]` because its paragraph goes on, and uses > `:term[…]` for one that another note defines; each use links to the [glossary](glossary.html). A :dfn[starter] is a culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria, kept alive in flour and water. Mine lives in a jar in the fridge and has been going since March. ## Feeding {#feeding} Once a week, and the day before a bake: 1. Keep 20 g of the old starter and throw the rest away. 2. Add 100 g of flour, four parts white to one part wholemeal rye, and 100 g of water at about 26°C: a starter at 100% :term[hydration]. 3. Mark the level on the jar with a rubber band and leave it out. It is ready when it has doubled and its top is domed, four to six hours later in a warm kitchen. A starter that has peaked and fallen back smells sharp, of vinegar, and is better fed again before it is used. ## The levain {#levain} :definition[A :dfn[levain] is a portion of the starter, fed for one bake and used up in it.] I build mine the evening before, from 20 g of starter, 100 g of flour and 100 g of water; by morning it is the 200 g that [my everyday loaf](hydration.html#formula) takes, and a little over. The jar goes back in the fridge untouched. A separate levain leaves the starter on its own schedule: a bake that goes wrong costs a levain, not the culture. ## When it is sluggish {#sluggish} - Feed it twice a day for two days, at room temperature. - Use more rye: it ferments faster than white flour. - Mind the water: chlorine slows the culture, and tap water left to stand overnight has lost most of it.
