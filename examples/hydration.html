:::meta title: Hydration and baker's percentages lang: en date: 2026-09-21 description: Writing a recipe against its flour, and what more water does to a dough. ::: # Hydration and baker's percentages > A note of the [bread notes](index.html), an example of the wiki add-on. A titled > `:::definition` block defines its title, a `:dfn[…]` the word in its sentence; the > `:term[…]` uses name terms the other notes define, and link to the [glossary](glossary.html). Bakers write a recipe against its flour, so that it scales by a single number. :::definition[Baker's percentage]{#bakers-percentage} An ingredient's weight as a share of the flour's, which counts as 100%: 750 g of water to 1000 g of flour is 75%. ::: ## Hydration {#hydration} A dough's :dfn[hydration] is its water as a baker's percentage of its flour. The :term[levain] counts too, since it is half flour and half water. - **65 to 70%** holds its shape on the bench and is easy to handle; the :term[crumb] is tighter. - **75 to 80%** is slack and sticky, with a more open crumb and a thinner crust. - **Over 80%** wants strong flour and practice: wet hands, and folds instead of kneading. Wholemeal flour drinks more water than white: add about 5% for every 20% of wholemeal in the flour. ## My everyday loaf {#formula} | Ingredient | Weight | Baker's % | |---|--:|--:| | White bread flour | 900 g | 90% | | Wholemeal rye | 100 g | 10% | | Water | 720 g | 72% | | Levain | 200 g | 20% | | Salt | 20 g | 2% | With the levain's 100 g of flour and 100 g of water, the whole dough is $\frac{720 + 100}{1000 + 100} \approx 75\%$ water. ## Autolyse {#autolyse} :definition[An :dfn[autolyse] is a rest of the flour and the water alone, before the levain and the salt go in.] Half an hour is enough for the flour to take up the water: the dough comes together with less kneading and stretches further. I hold back 20 g of the water and add it with the salt and the levain, at the start of the :term[bulk fermentation].
