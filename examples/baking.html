An hour before the loaf comes out of the fridge, after [the proof](fermentation.html#proof), the Dutch oven goes into the oven at 250°C, lid on. ## Scoring {#scoring} :dfn[Scoring] is cutting the top of the loaf just before it goes in, so that it opens where you choose instead of tearing at its weakest point. One long cut a few millimetres deep, with a razor blade held at a low angle, gives an ear: a flap of crust that lifts and browns. ## Steam and oven spring {#oven-spring} :dfn[Oven spring] is the loaf's last rise, in the first minutes of baking, as the gas in it expands and the yeast has a final burst before the heat kills it. It needs a moist surface: under the lid, the loaf's own steam keeps the crust soft long enough to stretch. - 20 minutes at 250°C with the lid on. - 25 minutes at 230°C with it off, until the crust is a deep brown and the base sounds hollow when tapped. A loaf that springs little was usually over-proofed ([the poke test](fermentation.html#proof) says when) or shaped too loosely. ## The :dfn[crumb] {#crumb} The inside of the loaf: the bread around its holes. An even, open crumb with a shine on the walls of its holes comes of a good :term[bulk fermentation]; big holes under the crust over dense bread mean it was cut short. A higher :term[hydration] opens it up too. Let the loaf cool for an hour before cutting it: the crumb is still setting, and cut warm it turns gummy.
