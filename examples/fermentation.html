:::meta title: Bulk fermentation and the proof lang: en date: 2026-10-03 description: From mixing to the oven: the folds, the first rise, shaping and a cold proof. ::: # Bulk fermentation and the proof > A note of the [bread notes](index.html), an example of the wiki add-on. One term is defined > by a `:dfn[…]` with `title=`, its canonical form; the `:term[…]` uses name terms the other > notes define, one of them inflected, and link to the [glossary](glossary.html). ## Bulk fermentation {#bulk} The :dfn[bulk fermentation] is the dough's first rise, in one piece, from mixing in the :term[levain] to shaping. It takes four to six hours at 24°C, longer in a cool kitchen: - Every 30 minutes for the first two hours, a set of folds: lift one side of the dough, fold it over, turn the bowl, four times round. - Then leave it alone until it has grown by half, is domed at the edges and shows bubbles on the sides of the bowl. The clock matters less than the dough. A dough at a high :term[hydration] needs more folds, and a gentler hand at the end. ## Shaping {#shaping} Turn the dough out, pre-shape it into a loose round and let it rest for 20 minutes. Then shape it tight: the tension of its surface holds it up in the oven. It goes into a floured basket, seam side up. ## The proof {#proof} :dfn[Proofing]{title=proof} is the last rise, in the shape the loaf will bake in. Mine is in the fridge overnight, 12 to 16 hours at 4°C. The cold slows the yeast more than the bacteria, so the bread tastes more sour, and a cold dough is easier to :term[score]{title=scoring}. The poke test says when it is ready: a floured finger pressed in leaves a dent that springs back slowly, halfway. A dent that springs back at once wants more time; one that stays is over-proofed, and the loaf will spread in the oven. It is still worth baking: flatter, with a closer :term[crumb], and as good toasted.
